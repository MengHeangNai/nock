# GlassDock

A glass-style replacement Dock for macOS 14 or later, with widgets for Now Playing, Battery and Clock. On macOS 26 Tahoe it uses Liquid Glass, and on earlier versions it uses a frosted material.

## Build and run

```bash
./scripts/build-app.sh
open build/GlassDock.app
```

Run the tests with `swift test`.

The build scripts sign the app with the local signing identity named `NotchUnlock Local Signing` when your keychain has it (set `GLASSDOCK_SIGN_IDENTITY` to use another identity name). That keeps the app's code identity the same on every rebuild, so Accessibility and Screen Recording only need to be allowed once per identity. When the identity is missing or `codesign` can't use it, the script prints a warning and signs ad hoc, and macOS asks for those permissions again after each rebuild. If a permission is stuck after the signing changed, reset it once with `tccutil reset Accessibility local.glassdock.app` (and `tccutil reset ScreenCapture local.glassdock.app`; the no-Dock build uses `local.glassdock.nodock`), then allow it again.

To keep it, move `build/GlassDock.app` to `/Applications`. Then turn on **Open at login** in Settings.

## Using it

- **Click an app** to open it or switch to it. The icon darkens while pressed and bounces until the app finishes launching. **Option-click** switches and hides the other apps; **Command-click** shows the app in Finder.
- **Right-click, Control-click or press and hold** an app for the same menu as the macOS Dock: its open windows, **Options** (Keep in Dock, Show in Finder), Show All Windows, Hide and Quit. Hold **Option** while the menu is open to get Hide Others and Force Quit.
- The menu shows **open windows** for running apps (needs Accessibility: **Settings → Dock → Show app windows in Dock menus**) and **recent documents** for apps that aren't running (needs Full Disk Access: **Settings → Dock → Show recent documents in Dock menus**). When the build is signed ad hoc (no local signing identity), macOS may ask again after each rebuild.
- **The menu bar icon** (dock symbol) opens **Settings** and **Quit**. GlassDock has no Dock icon of its own.
- **Several displays:** the Dock appears on every connected display, each with its own auto-hide, magnification and click-through, and all share the same apps and settings. To keep it on the main display (the one with the menu bar) only, choose **Settings → Dock → Show Dock on → Main display only**. Plugging in or unplugging a display, changing its resolution or arrangement, or waking the Mac adds, removes and moves the Docks by itself.
- **Settings:** icon size, magnification and its amount, which displays show the Dock, auto-hide, which widgets to show, whether to show running apps, adding, ordering and removing apps, hiding the macOS Dock, the Island on displays without a notch, the Island finish, hiding the Island in full-screen apps, the notch pet, the music visualizer, the Island's lyrics, system HUD and system event notifications, keyboard sounds, and open at login.
- **Now Playing** follows Music and Spotify, and also any other app that reports what it plays to macOS, such as YouTube or SoundCloud in Chrome, Safari, Arc or Firefox. The first time you use the playback buttons for Music or Spotify, macOS asks permission for GlassDock to control that app. Other apps need no permission. Music or Spotify playing always wins over another app.
- On first launch, GlassDock adds the apps from your current macOS Dock.

## Island (notch panel)

- **Hover over the notch** to open the panel. On Macs without a notch, and on every external display, hover over the pill at the top center of the screen. If the pill gets in the way, choose **Settings → Island → Island on displays without a notch**: **Show on hover** draws nothing there until you hover over that spot (the volume HUD and notifications still appear), and **Off** removes the Island from those displays. Displays with a notch always keep theirs. Turn on **Settings → Island → Hide in full-screen apps** (off by default) to hide the Island on any display showing a full-screen app: no pill, live activities, hover or notifications there, and an open Island closes. The volume and brightness HUD still shows, and other displays keep their Island. It comes back when you leave full screen. Choose how the Island looks under **Settings → Island → Island finish**: **Black** (the default) matches the notch, **Liquid Glass** makes the whole Island translucent glass with a dark tint so text stays readable (a blurred material before macOS 26), and **Dynamic Glass** keeps the notch band black and melts into glass below it, so the closed Island still looks black. In Low Power Mode the Island is always black. Every connected display has its own Island, and only one is open at a time: opening one closes the other. Move the mouse away or click elsewhere to close it.
- **Tabs, left of the notch:** Home, Now Playing, Lyrics, Timer, Battery, Up Next, Shelf, Clipboard and Notes.
- **Tabs, right of the notch:** Terminal, AI Usage, Color Picker, Keep Awake, Downloads, Devices, Stats, Windows and Keyboard. Hide any of them under **Settings → Island Tabs**.
- **Pin window:** the **Windows** tab lists the open windows of your apps on every display (not GlassDock's own, and no menu bar items, tiny windows or other apps' panels), each with the app icon, app name, window title, a pin button, a PiP button (see **PiP window**) and a menu. **Pin** keeps that real window in front of other apps' windows; pinned windows are listed first with a pin badge, and **Unpin all** releases them. The newest pin stays on top of older ones. The menu moves and resizes the window to the **left half**, **right half**, a **corner quarter**, the **center** (60% of the screen) or **maximize** (the visible area, below the menu bar and beside the Dock), or sends it to the **next display** with the same relative position and size. Pinning does not record the screen or mirror the window, so it needs no Screen Recording permission, only **Accessibility** (Privacy & Security → Accessibility; the tab shows a button for it until you allow it, and notices within a couple of seconds when you do, without a restart). It reads window titles through Accessibility too. macOS has no public way to change another app's window level, so pinning is best-effort: when a window of another app covers a pinned window, GlassDock brings the pinned window forward, and its app takes keyboard focus, because macOS only lets a window come forward over other apps when its app becomes active. A pinned window that no other app's window covers (for example one parked in a corner) is never touched and never takes focus. GlassDock checks right away when you switch apps or Spaces, change window focus, or press or release the mouse, and about ten times a second while something is pinned, so a pinned window comes back within a fraction of a second after you click another window; it waits while you drag (the pointer moved a few points since the press, or the button was held for about 0.4 seconds) so it never fights a drag, but not during a plain click; it does not raise while you use the Island or GlassDock is the active app, and waits at least 0.1 seconds between raises. If macOS refuses to bring a window forward, its row in the Windows tab says so with the Accessibility error code. The pinned window can flicker briefly, typing in the app you were using may be interrupted, and it may not stay above windows that macOS itself keeps on top. Windows in full-screen Spaces can't be pinned, minimized windows can't be pinned or moved, and a pin ends when the window closes or its app quits. Pins are not kept after GlassDock quits. Nothing runs for pinning while no window is pinned.
- **PiP window:** the PiP button on a row of the **Windows** tab opens a Picture-in-Picture panel, like the video PiP in Safari or Chrome: GlassDock's own floating panel shows a live view of that window (up to 30 frames per second, captured with ScreenCaptureKit). The panel is always on top, even over full-screen apps, and follows you to every Space, so it never flickers or hides when you switch desktops; drag it to any display and resize it (its shape stays the window's shape, 160 points wide at least). It opens at about 320 points wide near the bottom-right corner of the display the window is on; open as many as you like (the row shows a badge and the tab offers **Close all PiPs**). Move the pointer over a PiP for its controls, which fade out after a moment: close, opacity (50% to 100%), size (Small, Medium, Large), **Crop** and **Bring window back**; double-click the picture for the same. **Crop** shows only part of the window, for example a video player, a chat box or a chart: click it and the PiP shows the whole window dimmed; drag a rectangle over it (at least 48 by 48 points), then resize it by its eight handles (corners and edges) or move it by dragging inside it, and confirm with **Done** or Return (**Cancel** or Escape keeps what you had). While you choose, clicks on the PiP go to the crop and not to the real window, and the top grip bar still drags the PiP. The PiP then captures only the selected area (not the whole window), takes the shape of that area and keeps its center, and clicks, scrolling and typing land on the matching spot of the real window. A **Reset crop** button appears in the controls while a crop is active and brings back the whole window. The crop is remembered as a fraction of the window, so it follows when you resize the real window, and it is forgotten when you close the PiP. To move a PiP, drag the grip bar along its top (it shows with the controls), or hold **Option** and drag from anywhere on the picture; nothing else moves it. To resize it, drag any corner (small grips show there with the controls) or the left, right or bottom edge: the aspect ratio stays, the opposite corner or edge stays put, and it stops at the display's edges; presses on a corner or edge never reach the real window. Moving works across displays and the PiP stays where you drop it, and plain clicks and drags on the picture still go to the real window. The real window stays where it is (see **Keep live** for the one exception), and the PiP stays open when you bring it forward. PiP needs **Screen Recording** access (Privacy & Security → Screen Recording): GlassDock asks only the first time you click a PiP button, never at launch, and until you allow it the Windows tab shows a note with a button for the setting and notices within a couple of seconds when you do (macOS may ask you to quit and reopen GlassDock first). The frames are only shown on screen; they are never saved or sent, and GlassDock records nothing else. Clicks, drags, scrolling and typing on a PiP are passed to the real window as events on a best-effort basis (it also needs **Accessibility**): some apps ignore them, and context menus, drag and drop and keyboard shortcuts may not work through the PiP. Minimized and full-screen windows can't get a PiP (the button is dimmed with the reason), a PiP closes when its window closes or its app quits, and PiPs are not kept after GlassDock quits. When the source window is hidden (Hide from the Dock menu or Cmd+H), minimized, or macOS stops drawing it (its frames stay blank for about half a second), the PiP stays open and keeps the last picture, dimmed, with a badge ("Window hidden", "Window minimized" or "Window not visible") and a **Show window** button that unhides or restores the real window and brings it forward; clicks, scrolling and typing on the dimmed picture are not passed on, but the drag bar and controls still work. When the window comes back the dim fades out and the picture is live again. GlassDock notices within about half a second (instantly for hiding and unhiding an app). Apps like Safari may stop drawing video while their window is fully covered by other windows, so the picture can freeze until the window is uncovered (Safari's own video Picture in Picture avoids this; **Keep live** avoids it too); GlassDock only dims the picture when the window is hidden, minimized or blank, never for a covered or static window. A window on another Space is not dimmed: the PiP follows you across Spaces and shows its live picture, or keeps the last one when macOS stops delivering frames for that Space. If a large block in the middle of the picture stays black for a few seconds while the rest of the window is not (checked about once a second on a coarse grid, never for a window that is black as a whole), GlassDock first switches that PiP once from capturing the window alone to capturing the display it is on, showing only that window, in case the browser's video layer is only drawn in the display's picture (it switches back if the picture then stops arriving); if the block stays black, the window's row in the **Windows** tab says "Part of this window looks black. Protected video (Netflix, Apple TV+, etc.) can't be copied by macOS." Nothing runs for PiP while none is open.
- **Keep live (experimental):** while a PiP is open, GlassDock moves the real window onto a hidden virtual screen, so macOS can't mark it covered and apps like Safari or Chrome keep drawing video; the PiP stays live even when other windows would hide the window. It uses a screen called **GlassDock Hidden Screen** (2560 by 1440, not mirrored, never the main display) that GlassDock creates when the first PiP needs it and removes when the last one closes; it shows up in **System Settings → Displays** while a PiP is open, and the mouse pointer can enter it by accident (push it back to a real display). GlassDock puts no Dock or Island there and leaves it out of the pin and snap menus. The window goes to the top-left of that screen (smaller if it is bigger than the screen), you use it through the PiP as usual, and its row in the **Windows** tab shows a small display icon ("Kept on hidden screen while in PiP"). The window goes back to its original display and frame when the PiP closes, when you click **Bring window back** (or double-click the picture, or **Show window** on a dimmed PiP; the PiP keeps streaming), when you turn the setting off, and when GlassDock quits normally; if that display is gone it goes inside the main display, and a window you moved elsewhere yourself stays where you put it. If the app moves the window away on its own, GlassDock moves it back at most once a second and gives up after three tries in a row, with a note in the Windows tab. If GlassDock crashes, macOS moves the window off the vanished screen by itself. Turn it on or off in **Settings → Island → Keep PiP windows playing (experimental)** (off by default; the change applies to open PiPs too). It relies on undocumented macOS features (private CoreGraphics virtual display classes) and may stop working after a macOS update; when it can't make the screen the Windows tab says "Keep live is not available on this Mac" and the PiP works as before. Limitations: full-screen, minimized and hidden windows are not moved ("This window can't be kept live"), and neither are windows of apps that refuse to move or whose window does not land on the hidden screen within about 0.3 seconds; the window is not on your screen while it is kept live.
- **Permissions:** Calendar is requested when you click **Connect Calendar**, and the Downloads folder when you open **Downloads**.
- **AI Usage** shows Codex's 5-hour and weekly limits, read from its local logs. For Claude Code it shows token counts from local logs. If you turn on **Show plan limits**, it also shows Claude's own limits: GlassDock reads Claude Code's sign-in from the Keychain (macOS asks first) and sends it only to Anthropic.
- **Notes** shows the notes from the Notes app on your Mac. Plain-text notes save back to Notes as you type. Notes with formatting or attachments are read-only in GlassDock, so nothing gets lost; use the open button to edit them in Notes. Pins are kept only in GlassDock. Deleting a note moves it to Recently Deleted in Notes.
- **Shelf actions** work on the selected files, or on all files if none are selected: Quick Look, Open, Show in Finder, Share, AirDrop, convert images (PNG, JPEG, HEIC), combine into a PDF, compress to .zip, and copy text from images and PDFs (text recognition runs on your Mac). New files are saved to Downloads.
- **Live activities** beside the notch: music playing (the bars move with the sound your Mac plays; macOS asks once for permission to record system audio, which GlassDock only measures and never stores or sends), a timer counting down, "Done" when the timer ends, the charge level when you plug in, and a **volume, brightness and keyboard backlight HUD**. The HUD replaces the macOS overlay: the Island grows below the notch (a pill on Macs without a notch) with a large icon, a label, a level bar and the percentage, for about 1.5 seconds, longer while you keep pressing. When the Island is open, the HUD floats over its content instead. The HUD covers the other live activities while it shows, then they return.
With several displays, live activities show beside the notch or pill on every display.
- **System event notifications:** a short HUD in the same place as the volume HUD (the Island drops below the notch or pill, or floats over the open Island) for **Bluetooth devices** connecting and disconnecting (device name, with an icon for AirPods, headphones, speaker, keyboard, mouse, trackpad or game controller), **Wi-Fi** connecting, disconnecting, turning on and turning off, **low battery** at 20% (orange) and 10% (red) while on battery, once each per discharge, and **Fully charged** once per charge. Each shows for about 2.6 seconds (3.2 for battery warnings) with a spring drop, a bouncing icon, and a pulse on warnings. Warnings go first, a few more wait in line, and the volume and brightness HUD always comes first: a notification waits and then starts again. Only real changes show: nothing appears for the state at launch, and events right after waking the Mac are skipped. With **Reduce Motion** on, it only fades. Each notice is announced to VoiceOver. It never shows the Wi-Fi network name (macOS keeps it behind Location access) or a device's battery level, and it never reads other apps' notifications. Turn it on or off, and Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and battery separately, under **Settings → Island → Show system event notifications**. macOS may ask once to allow Bluetooth.
- **Volume and brightness keys:** with **Settings → Island → Replace system volume & brightness HUD** on (the default), GlassDock takes over the volume, mute, display brightness and keyboard backlight keys and makes the change itself, so macOS shows no overlay. Each press moves 1/16 of the range; **Shift-Option** moves 1/64. **Option** alone (or Command or Control) is left to macOS, so Option plus a volume key still opens Sound settings. Brightness changes the display under the mouse when it has built-in brightness control; on any other display (most external monitors) it changes the built-in screen instead, as the macOS keys do, and GlassDock shows the HUD for it. Keys that GlassDock cannot handle, such as brightness with the built-in screen unavailable (lid closed) or backlight on a Mac without one, go to macOS untouched. GlassDock watches the keys at the HID level, before the session event taps, so macOS does not see the keys it takes. Brightness and backlight use private macOS frameworks (DisplayServices and CoreBrightness) that Apple may change. GlassDock makes no volume feedback click.
- **Permission for the keys:** the Island must be on, and GlassDock needs **Accessibility** access (Privacy & Security → Accessibility; the Settings row has a button for it) to take over the keys. If it can't get it, or macOS also asks for **Input Monitoring**, GlassDock takes over nothing: the macOS overlay stays and the Island only shows volume changes. While Accessibility is missing, GlassDock checks every 2 seconds and takes over the keys as soon as you allow it, without turning the setting off and on or restarting. Turning the setting off, turning the Island off, or quitting GlassDock gives the keys back to macOS. Because the build is signed ad hoc, macOS may ask again after each rebuild.
- **Notch pet:** a small mint-green creature drawn entirely in SwiftUI (no image files) sits in the left wing of the collapsed Island while no live activity, HUD or notification shows; the Island grows by a compact wing on both sides (each about half the width of a music or timer wing, so the shape stays centered on the notch and balanced, with the pet on the left and the right wing plain black), and the hover area grows with it. It follows the other Island settings: it is hidden wherever the idle pill is hidden (**Show on hover** on displays without a notch, **Hide in full-screen apps**) and it makes way for music, timer and charging wings. Its mood follows the Mac: it **sleeps** after 2 minutes without keyboard or mouse input (checked every 3 seconds), gets **tired** at 20% battery or less while unplugged, does a **happy hop** for 4 seconds when you plug in or add a file to the Shelf, and blinks now and then otherwise. While music plays it **dances** next to the playback controls on the Home tab instead (the music wing takes the notch spot), but not on the Now Playing tab. It grows with the days since GlassDock first ran: a baby for the first 7 days, young until day 30, then grown. With **Reduce Motion** on it shows still poses. Turn it off under **Settings → Island → Show notch pet** (on by default).
- **Music visualizer:** the open **Now Playing** tab shows a live visualizer to the right of the song title, drawn with SwiftUI (no image files) from the same four sound bands as the music bars. Click it to switch between **Bars** (a spectrum), **Wave** (a waveform line that follows the loudness of the last two seconds) and **Rings** (concentric rings that pulse outward on each beat); GlassDock remembers the style. Its two colors come from the album art (a neutral blue-gray without art, or for gray art), worked out once per track. It records system audio only while it is on screen and the song is playing, and it shares the same recording as the music bars; if macOS refuses the audio permission it moves with a gentle built-in motion instead. It draws at up to 30 frames per second, and with **Reduce Motion** on it shows one still, dimmed frame and records nothing. It is hidden from VoiceOver apart from a button that names the style and changes it. Turn it off under **Settings → Island → Show music visualizer** (on by default); the Now Playing tab then looks as before.
- **Karaoke lyrics:** when a song has time-synced lyrics, the **Lyrics** tab shows them in karaoke view by default: the current line is large and centered, with the previous and next lines small and dimmed above and below, and its words fill in one by one as they are sung. The lyrics source only times whole lines, so GlassDock shares each line's time between its words by their length (a line stops filling after about 0.3 seconds per character when the next line starts much later). Long lines shrink to fit, up to two lines. Behind the lyrics is a soft gradient from the album art's two colors (neutral without art), blended slowly when the song changes. The round button at the top right of the lyrics switches between **Karaoke** and **List** (the scrolling list where clicking a line jumps to it); GlassDock remembers the choice. Songs with only plain lyrics keep the plain text. The karaoke view redraws at up to 30 frames per second, only while the Lyrics tab is open and the song is playing. With **Reduce Motion** on, the whole current line is highlighted at once and lines fade instead of sliding. VoiceOver reads the current line.
- **Shelf:** drag files onto the notch to keep them there. Drag them back out to use them anywhere, or double-click to open one. The Shelf stores links to the files; it doesn't move or copy them.
- **Clipboard:** keeps your last 100 text copies in memory, and saves only the ones you pin. It skips copies that password managers mark as private. Click an item to copy it again.
- **Clipboard transforms:** click the wand icon on an item, or right-click it and choose **Transform**, to put a changed copy on the clipboard. The copy is added to the history like any other copy. The menu lists only what applies to that item: **Format JSON** and **Minify JSON** (when the text is a JSON object or array; text that looks like JSON but doesn't parse shows "Not valid JSON"), **Remove tracking parameters** (for http and https links; removes `utm_*`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, `mc_eid`, `igshid`, `ref` and similar, and keeps other parameters), **Trim whitespace**, **Collapse blank lines**, **UPPERCASE**, **lowercase**, **Title Case**, **Base64 encode and decode**, and **URL encode and decode**. Images are not kept in the clipboard history, so there is no text recognition for clipboard images yet.
- **Keyboard sounds:** plays a mechanical switch sound for every keystroke in any app (off by default; needs **Accessibility** for keys typed in other apps). The **Keyboard** tab has the on/off switch, volume and the list of sounds: three built-in sounds generated on your Mac (Clicky, Tactile, Thock) plus any **Mechvibes sound packs** you add. Click a sound to use it, or its play button to hear a short demo, even while keyboard sounds are off. To add real recorded switches, click **Get Packs** (opens mechvibes.com/sound-packs), download a pack, unzip it and put its folder (the one with `config.json`) into `~/Library/Application Support/GlassDock/Soundpacks/` (**Open Folder** opens it), then click the rescan button. Packs from the current Mechvibes (with key-up sounds) and classic Mechvibes packs both work, with WAV, MP3, M4A or OGG audio; a pack that can't be read is dimmed with the reason. No sound packs ship with GlassDock. GlassDock only uses which key was pressed to pick its sound and never reads or records what you type.
- **Mouse sounds:** plays a click for the left, right and middle mouse buttons and their release, separately from keyboard sounds (off by default; works whether or not keyboard sounds are on). Switch the **Keyboard** tab to **Mouse** to choose **Off**, **Match keyboard** (the keyboard sound, pitched up), three built-in clicks (Soft, Crisp, Hollow) or your own mouse packs, each with a play button, and set the mouse volume and **Scroll sounds** (off by default; at most one tick every 50 ms, trackpad momentum is ignored). For a mouse pack, make a folder in `~/Library/Application Support/GlassDock/Soundpacks/Mouse/` (**Open Folder** in Mouse mode opens it) holding `click.wav` and optionally `release.wav`, `right.wav`, `middle.wav` and `scroll.wav`; WAV, MP3, M4A, AIFF, CAF or OGG files work, and any missing sound plays the click instead. Mechvibes mouse packs work too: unzip one into its own folder there and GlassDock reads its `config.json` (Mechvibes v2, `single` or `multi`), using `MouseLeft` for the click (required), `MouseRight`, `MouseMiddle` and a `MouseWheel…`/`Wheel…` entry for scrolling; a button or wheel the pack lacks plays the `MouseLeft` sound, and a button's second timing is its release, so a pack without release timings is silent on release. A folder with a `config.json` is always read as a Mechvibes pack. **Settings → Keyboard & Mouse Sounds** has the same keyboard and mouse choices plus pitch variation and muting while music plays, which apply to both.
- Turn Dock and Island on or off separately under **Settings → Modules**.

## Hiding the macOS Dock

The setting **Hide the macOS Dock while GlassDock is running** turns on auto-hide for the system Dock and sets a very long reveal delay. When you quit GlassDock, it restores your original values. If GlassDock crashes or is force-quit, it restores them on the next launch. To restore them by hand:

```bash
defaults delete com.apple.dock autohide-delay; killall Dock
```

| `Displays.swift` | Display IDs and UUIDs for screens and which displays get a Dock (**Show Dock on**), the screens GlassDock's own windows use (never the hidden virtual display, found by its vendor and product number), and a screen as an Accessibility-coordinate area; tested in `Tests/GlassDockTests/` |
| `DockView.swift` | The dock bar, magnification math, app and Trash icons |
| `DockItemInteraction.swift` | Clicks, press-and-hold and the right-click menu for Dock items, the app's window list (Accessibility), the name label shape |
| `Widgets.swift` | Now Playing, Battery and Clock widgets |
| `DockModel.swift` | Kept and running apps from NSWorkspace, plus app actions |
| `NowPlaying.swift` | Now Playing: Music and Spotify (distributed notifications and AppleScript), other apps and browsers (MediaRemote) |
| `GlassDockMediaRemote/` | Helper library that reads and controls MediaRemote. macOS only allows Apple-signed programs to use it, so GlassDock loads the library inside `/usr/bin/perl`. The build scripts put it next to the app's executable |
| `AudioLevels.swift` | Reads the level of the sound being played (Core Audio system tap) and splits it into four bands for the music bars and the music visualizer in the Island |
| `SystemHUD.swift` | The HUD's pure rules: media key decoding, step sizes, modifier behaviour, symbol, label and percentage; tested in `Tests/GlassDockTests/` |
| `SystemHUDController.swift` | Turns the media keys into changes and publishes what the Island HUD shows; installs and removes the key tap as Accessibility access, the setting and the Island module change |
| `SystemNotifications.swift` | The notifications' pure rules: the notification model, the queue (priority, duplicates, a short line, the HUD going first), battery thresholds, Wi-Fi changes, Bluetooth connection tracking and device icons; tested in `Tests/GlassDockTests/` |
| `SystemNotificationController.swift` | Starts and stops the Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and battery watchers from the settings, feeds the queue, times each notification and announces it to VoiceOver |
| `MediaKeyTap.swift` | The active event tap on the volume, brightness and backlight keys |
| `SystemVolume.swift` | Output volume and mute: Core Audio reads, writes and property listeners on the default output device |
| `Brightness.swift` | Built-in display brightness (private DisplayServices) and keyboard backlight (private CoreBrightness), loaded at run time |
| `Monitors.swift` | Battery (IOKit), Bluetooth connections (IOBluetooth), Wi-Fi state (CoreWLAN), hiding the system Dock |
| `MusicVisualizer.swift` | The music visualizer's pure rules: the styles (Bars, Wave, Rings), the rolling level history, how it becomes bar, wave and ring levels, and the two accent colors taken from album art; tested in `Tests/GlassDockTests/` |
| `KaraokeLyrics.swift` | The karaoke lyrics' pure rules: the view mode (Karaoke, List), how a line's time is shared between its words by length, when a line stops being sung, and how far each word is sung; tested in `Tests/GlassDockTests/` |
| `NotchPet.swift` | The notch pet's pure rules: which mood it is in (idle, sleeping, dancing, tired, happy) and its growth stage by days since first launch; tested in `Tests/GlassDockTests/` |
| `NotchPetMonitor.swift` | Watches input idle time, music, battery and the Shelf, and publishes the pet's mood and stage |
| `DockSettings.swift` | Preferences saved in UserDefaults |
